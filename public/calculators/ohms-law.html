About Ohm's Law

Ohm's Law is one of the most fundamental laws in electrical engineering, discovered by German physicist Georg Simon Ohm in 1827. It states that the current through a conductor between two points is directly proportional to the voltage across the two points.

Key Formulas:

  • V = I × R (Voltage = Current × Resistance)
  • I = V / R (Current = Voltage / Resistance)
  • R = V / I (Resistance = Voltage / Current)
  • P = V × I (Power = Voltage × Current)

WORKED EXAMPLE

If a circuit has Voltage = 12V and Resistance = 4Ω, then Current = 12 ÷ 4 = 3 Amperes. Power = 12 × 3 = 36 Watts.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Q: What is Ohm's Law?

A: Ohm's Law states that the current through a conductor is directly proportional to the voltage and inversely proportional to the resistance: V = I × R.

Q: Can I use this for AC circuits?

A: Ohm's Law applies directly to DC circuits. For AC circuits, use impedance (Z) instead of resistance (R).

Q: What units does this calculator use?

A: Voltage in Volts (V), Current in Amperes (A), Resistance in Ohms (Ω), and Power in Watts (W).

Q: What if I enter all three values?

A: The calculator uses the first two valid values and verifies consistency with the third.
