About Torque

Torque is a measure of the rotational force applied to an object. It's calculated as the product of force and the perpendicular distance from the axis of rotation.

τ = F × r
Where τ is torque, F is force, and r is the lever arm distance

Common Applications

  • Automotive: Engine torque specifications, wheel lug nuts
  • Manufacturing: Bolt tightening specifications
  • Aerospace: Critical fastener torque requirements
  • Machinery: Motor and gear specifications

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS

Q: What is the most common torque unit?

A: Newton-meter (N·m) is the SI standard. Automotive industry often uses lb·ft. Mechanical engineering may use kgf·m.

Q: How do I convert lb·ft to N·m?

A: Multiply lb·ft by 1.3558 to get N·m. Example: 100 lb·ft = 135.58 N·m.

Q: What torque does a car engine produce?

A: Typical car engines produce 150-400 N·m. Performance cars may produce 500-1000 N·m. Heavy trucks 2000+ N·m.

Q: What is the torque of a human hand?

A: A firm hand grip can produce approximately 10-30 N·m of torque, depending on the person and tool used.
